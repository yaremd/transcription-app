Seal — two logo routes

Both earless and mouthless. Both use only forest #0A2A21 and lime #D3F36B. Both are built on the same 240-unit grid, so whichever wins can be handed to a studio as exact coordinates rather than a picture. Pick one and push it — don't average them.

Route B

The struck impression

A solid disc with the face punched clean through — which is literally what a wax seal is. The holes are transparent, so the same file is the app icon, the menu-bar template glyph and the favicon. Mass, not line: it survives being shrunk, tinted, or printed in one colour.

Route C

The unbroken seal

One closed contour of constant weight that never breaks — it is the head and the tamper-evident ring at the same time. Line and mass are the only two values in the whole mark. It reads as a seal of office before it reads as an animal, which is the more institutional of the two answers.

Head to head

Where they actually differ

Route B — at 16 px, and full size
Route C — at 16 px, and full size
B is mass; C is line. B holds at any size, tints to a single arbitrary colour without losing the face, and never needs a second drawing except to drop the whiskers below 24 px — the durable choice for something that lives in a menu bar all day. C carries the better story and looks more like a seal of office, but a stroked contour thins to nothing at small sizes, so it needs a genuinely separate small cut and it will always be the weaker favicon. If Seal is mostly going to be seen small, that is the decision.
